Lesson plan generator with quote grounding

Status
Pilot
Role
Sole engineer

K–8. Grade bands K–2, 3–5, 6–8

Purchased trade books and public-domain texts

Problem

Lesson planning for a knowledge-building ELA curriculum is time-intensive and uneven in quality across a network. Teachers need plans that cite the source text accurately, align to stated instructional expectations, and are ready to teach without further preparation.

Interface

Mode 1 inputUnit overview, lesson number, grade band.
Mode 1 outputComplete teach-ready lesson plan against a fixed template.
Mode 2 inputAn existing lesson plan the teacher is preparing to deliver.
Mode 2 outputMulti-turn internalization coaching conversation.

Core constraint: quote accuracy at scale

The binding requirement is not plan quality in the abstract. It is that every passage a plan cites is a verbatim quotation from the actual text. A plan that quotes a book approximately is unusable — the teacher reads it aloud in front of a class, against the real page.

Model memory of a text is not an acceptable source. Source books are parsed into chapters, chunked, and supplied to generation as context. Quotations are drawn from supplied text, not recalled. This is the difference between a system a teacher can carry into a classroom and a demo.

Pipeline

  1. Extract. Purchased book text to structured chapters. Detailed below.
  2. Chunk. Chapters segmented into exactly n lesson-scale units, each carrying an index, a title, and a chapter reference.
  3. Distill. Per-chapter summaries and a quick pass over the full text.
  4. Overview. Unit overview generation — arc, virtue focus, standards alignment, lesson count.
  5. Generate. Lesson plan against the template, grounded in that lesson's chunk plus curriculum resources.
  6. Refine. Teacher-directed revision applied to a generated plan.

Extraction

Source books arrive as unstructured text with no reliable format. A purchased trade book, a public-domain transcription, and a scanned reader each mark chapter boundaries differently, and getting boundaries wrong corrupts every downstream quotation. Extraction is a cascade of parsing strategies, tried in order; the first to yield two or more chapters wins, and the strategy that won is recorded against the book.

#StrategyDetects
1TOC-anchoredA CONTENTS block. Titles are read from it, then located in the body after the table of contents ends.
2All-caps headingsMulti-word and single-word capitalized heading lines.
3Chapter-NCHAPTER, STORY, or PART followed by an arabic or roman numeral.
4Markdown# and ## headings.
5Single chapterWhole text as one chapter. Always succeeds; guarantees the stage cannot hard-fail.

Extraction guards

The failure mode that matters is not a crash. It is a parse that succeeds and is wrong.

GuardPrevents
A heading followed by fewer than 200 characters of prose is discarded Table-of-contents lines and running heads registering as chapters
Duplicate titles resolve to their last occurrence A chapter anchoring to its TOC entry rather than the chapter itself, which would truncate every chapter to the length of a TOC line
Chapters carry start and end offsets into the raw text Untraceable quotations. Any cited passage resolves to a position in the source.
Chapter references resolve by normalized exact match, then substring Brittle failure on punctuation and case differences between a lesson's reference and the book's own heading
A multi-reference lookup returns what matched; null only if nothing matched One unmatched reference discarding the text of its matched siblings
Parse output persisted as JSON Re-parsing on every generation. Downstream text extraction is programmatic and cheap.

Grounding resources

Generation is conditioned on the network's own instructional documents rather than general pedagogical knowledge. Resources are stored records injected into the prompt, not fine-tuned weights, so a change to an instructional norm takes effect on the next run.

ResourceFunction
Teaching expectationsInstructional norms the plan must conform to
Narration protocolCore comprehension method
Socratic seminar protocolDiscussion structure
Virtue formation frameworkCharacter objectives per lesson
Assessment and exit ticketsFormative assessment design

Human adversarial review

No generated lesson is used unreviewed. Every lesson across all four units — 25 to 30 per unit — was read by a human reviewer and passed before entering the curriculum. Review is adversarial by design: the reviewer's task is to attack the plan, not approve it.

Defect classExample
Scaffolding slopeIndependent practice escalating faster than the unit arc supports; early lessons requiring writing where drawing and oral narration belong
Arc placementA summative writing task positioned mid-unit rather than as the culminating piece
Objective mismatchA cited passage that does not support the stated comprehension objective
Quotation fidelityAny passage not matching the source text verbatim

Corrections are captured against the specific lesson and retained. The review record is the system's improvement signal: domain experts generate correction data as a byproduct of doing their normal work, at a volume no single prompt author could produce, and each pass raises the floor for the next generation run. Reviewer time is spent on judgment rather than drafting — which is the entire economic argument for the system.

Mode 2 does not need the stronger model. Assigning models per mode rather than per system is what puts network-wide deployment inside a budget that does not require a procurement conversation.

Limitations

  • Pilot stage. Four units built; not yet deployed network-wide.
  • Quote fidelity is bounded by extraction quality. A poorly digitized source degrades grounding before generation ever runs.
  • Every lesson requires human review to ship. Review is the throughput constraint, by design — the system reduces drafting cost, not approval cost.
  • Requires a machine-readable source text.
