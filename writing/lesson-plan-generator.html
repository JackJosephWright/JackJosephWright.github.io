Lesson plan generator with quote grounding

Problem

Lesson planning for a knowledge-building ELA curriculum is time-intensive and uneven in quality across a network. Teachers need plans that cite the source text accurately, align to stated instructional expectations, and are ready to teach without further preparation.

Interface

Mode 1 inputUnit overview, lesson number, grade band.
Mode 1 outputComplete teach-ready lesson plan against a fixed template.
Mode 2 inputAn existing lesson plan the teacher is preparing to deliver.
Mode 2 outputMulti-turn internalization coaching conversation.

Core constraint: quote accuracy at scale

The binding requirement is not plan quality in the abstract. It is that every passage a plan cites is a verbatim quotation from the actual text. A plan that quotes a book approximately is unusable — the teacher reads it aloud in front of a class, against the real page.

Model memory of a text is not an acceptable source. Source books are parsed into chapters, chunked, and supplied to generation as context. Quotations are drawn from supplied text, not recalled. This is the difference between a system a teacher can carry into a classroom and a demo.

Pipeline

  1. Ingest. Source text parsed into chapters.
  2. Chunk. Chapter segmentation into lesson-scale units with titles and indices.
  3. Distill. Chapter summaries and a quick pass over the full text.
  4. Overview. Unit overview generation — arc, virtue focus, standards alignment, lesson count.
  5. Generate. Lesson plan against the template, grounded in the chunk for that lesson plus curriculum resources.
  6. Refine. Teacher-directed revision applied to a generated plan.

Grounding resources

Generation is conditioned on the network's own instructional documents rather than general pedagogical knowledge. Resources are stored records injected into the prompt, not fine-tuned weights, so a change to an instructional norm takes effect on the next run.

ResourceFunction
Teaching expectationsInstructional norms the plan must conform to
Narration protocolCore comprehension method
Socratic seminar protocolDiscussion structure
Virtue formation frameworkCharacter objectives per lesson
Assessment and exit ticketsFormative assessment design

Human adversarial review

Generated plans are reviewed by teachers who challenge them directly — scaffolding that progresses too fast, a writing task misplaced in the unit arc, a passage that does not support the stated objective. Corrections are captured against the specific lesson and fed back into generation.

This is the mechanism that makes the system improve rather than plateau. Domain experts produce correction signal as a byproduct of using the tool, at a volume no single author could generate. Reviewer time is spent on judgment, not on drafting.

Deployment economics

Mode 2 does not need the stronger model. Assigning models per mode rather than per system is what puts network-wide deployment inside a budget that does not require a procurement conversation.

Limitations

  • Pilot stage. Not yet deployed network-wide.
  • Quote fidelity depends on source-text parse quality; poorly digitized texts degrade grounding.
  • Correction capture is per-lesson and manual. No automated promotion of corrections into prompt changes.
  • Coverage is limited to units with a machine-readable source text.
